Street-level coverage

The overview map measures coverage by area: what share of a regular grid of sample points around a city has street-level imagery. This page measures it by road network instead: what fraction of a city's streets have actually been driven.

Each collection walks every edge of the city's OpenStreetMap street network, samples on-street points every ~15 m along the centerline, and asks the imagery provider for the nearest panorama at each point. A sample counts as covered when a panorama is found within the match distance, so coverage is fractional per street — a road that is half driven reports 50%, not "covered". The headline number below weights each street by its length.

Street-level collection runs on the same rolling schedule as the grid, so cities appear here as they are walked and are refreshed each cycle. Google Street View costs one metadata request per sample point (a large city runs to a few hundred thousand), while Mapillary is derived from its vector-tile census — so Mapillary cities fill in faster.
