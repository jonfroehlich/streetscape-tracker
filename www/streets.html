Street-level coverage

What fraction of a city's streets have actually been driven — a road network measure, where the overview map and Grid measure area instead.

How to read this table

Each collection walks every edge of the city's OpenStreetMap street network, samples on-street points every ~15 m along the centerline, and asks the provider for the nearest panorama at each. A sample counts as covered when a panorama is found within the match distance, so coverage is fractional per street — a road half driven reports 50%, not "covered" — and the headline number weights each street by its length.

Each city is one row, with a sub-column per provider: every provider walks the same sample points on the same frozen network, so their numbers are directly comparable. The Δ column gives one fixed difference (Mapillary − GSV) rather than a moving "best − rest", so its sign means the same thing in every row; a further provider gets its own sub-columns but no Δ. Click any provider's cell to open that provider's walk. The Network selector is separate for the opposite reason — "Roads" and "Roads + paths" divide by different street-km totals, so they are never shown side by side.

Collection runs on the same rolling schedule as the grid, so cities appear here as they are walked. Google Street View costs one metadata request per sample point (a large city runs to a few hundred thousand), while Mapillary is derived from its vector-tile census — so Mapillary cities fill in faster. Each numeric filter in the sidebar carries a histogram of where the rows actually sit, drawn over everything the other controls have selected. The address bar always carries the view you are looking at.
